1 5.3.1 | Gas exchange in the human respiratory system occurs across the micro-thin squamous epithelial walls of the alveoli and the surrounding pulmonary capillary network, where oxygen diffuses down its partial pressure gradient into the blood and carbon dioxide diffuses from blood into alveolar air. | The extensive capillary network covering the alveoli provides the thin, highly vascularized surface required for rapid respiratory gas exchange. | The trachea is a conducting airway reinforced by cartilaginous rings lined with ciliated epithelium; no gas exchange occurs across its walls. | Bronchioles are conducting air passages leading to alveolar ducts and do not exchange respiratory gases directly with alveoli. | Trachea and bronchioles are conducting airways that transport air into and out of the lungs; neither participates in gas exchange.
2 5.4.1 | In emphysema, chronic exposure to respiratory irritants like cigarette smoke destroys elastic fibres and alveolar septa. As adjacent alveolar walls break down, abnormally enlarged air spaces form, drastically reducing the total surface area available for gas exchange and trapping air upon exhalation, which causes severe breathlessness (dyspnoea). | Coughing is an associated symptom or protective reflex in respiratory conditions, but it does not directly cause the breathlessness seen in emphysema. | Destruction of alveolar septa leads to abnormally enlarged alveolar air spaces and loss of surface area and elasticity, resulting in breathlessness. | In obstructive pulmonary disorders, bronchiolar walls typically become inflamed, thickened, and obstructed by mucus rather than thin. | Emphysema is a primary pulmonary disease affecting lung parenchyma and gas exchange, not a primary cardiovascular pump failure.
3 5.1.1 | Respiration (cellular respiration) is a complex metabolic intracellular pathway involving catabolic, enzyme-catalyzed chemical reactions (such as glycolysis, Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation) that break down organic food molecules (glucose) to synthesize ATP. Gaseous exchange, on the other hand, is a purely physical process of diffusion across respiratory surfaces. | Cellular respiration consists of a stepwise series of biochemical reactions that oxidize organic molecules to release chemical energy stored as ATP. | Physical gaseous exchange depends strictly on partial pressure and concentration gradients for diffusion, whereas respiration is enzyme-driven metabolic catabolism. | The lungs and respiratory passages mediate mechanical ventilation and physical gaseous exchange, whereas cellular respiration occurs inside cells throughout the body. | Cellular respiration is a universal biochemical process occurring in all living organisms, including bacteria, protists, plants, and animals.
4 12.2.2 | Xerophytes are plants adapted to dry, arid environments such as deserts. To survive severe water scarcity and high transpiration rates, they possess specialized adaptations including sunken stomata in deep pits (which trap moist still air), fleshy succulent stems or leaves for storing water, and a thick impermeable waxy cuticle covering their epidermis. | Sunken stomata, succulent water-storing tissues, and a thick waxy cuticle are classic xerophytic adaptations for water conservation in arid desert habitats. | Alpine/mountain plants often exhibit dwarfism or rosette forms to withstand cold and wind, but succulent organs specifically characterize arid desert xerophytes. | Freshwater plants (hydrophytes) have thin or absent cuticles, large air cavities (aerenchyma), and abundant stomata on upper leaf surfaces. | Marine plants (halophytes) possess adaptations for high salinity, such as salt-secreting glands and high osmotic potentials, rather than typical desert succulent organs.
5 12.5.6 | Both the human kidneys and a haemodialysis machine regulate and maintain the correct electrolyte and mineral balance (such as sodium, potassium, and chloride concentrations) and pH in the blood while eliminating toxic metabolic wastes (like urea) down a concentration gradient into the dialysate or urine. | Neither kidneys nor dialysis machines reabsorb large proteins into blood; intact plasma proteins are impermeable and remain in the vascular circulation. | Macromolecules (such as plasma albumin and blood cells) are prevented from passing through the semi-permeable membrane in both dialysis and normal kidneys. | Both dialysis machines and normal human kidneys regulate and balance electrolytes and minerals in the blood to maintain homeostatic equilibrium. | Deoxygenation of blood is caused by tissue cellular respiration in body capillary beds, not by the excretory filtration of kidneys or dialysis machines.

17 16.4.4 | An individual with genotype QqRr is heterozygous at two independently assorting gene loci (n = 2). The number of genetically distinct gamete types formed by independent assortment in meiosis is given by 2^n = 2^2 = 4, specifically QR, Qr, qR, and qr. | A homozygous individual (such as QQRR) produces only 1 gamete type, whereas a dihybrid produces 4. | A monohybrid individual (heterozygous at one locus, e.g. QqRR) produces 2 gamete types (2^1 = 2). | Independent assortment of the two heterozygous gene pairs (Qq and Rr) yields 2^2 = 4 distinct gamete combinations (QR, Qr, qR, qr). | A trihybrid individual (heterozygous at 3 independent gene loci, 2^3) produces 8 gamete types.
